GranthStudio facts used in every principle:
- One shot is one held picture for 6 s. There is no limb, walking, lip or face motion inside a shot.
- The camera move is fixed per preset (zoom gains: 01 0.28, 02 0.13, 03 0.20, 05/07/10 0.14, 08 0.25, 09 0.13). Each preset moves one way.
- Presets 01, 04, 06 and 08 always add a warm halo.
- Transitions are cut, slide or pan_bridge.

## DP1. Cause first, response second

| Field | Content |
|---|---|
| What it helps achieve | The viewer sees why someone reacts. Cause and effect read in order without narration having to explain them. |
| When to use | An ovi (or two adjacent ovis) states an action or sight AND a named person's response to it. |
| When NOT to use | The ovi states no response: do not invent one (rule F3). The response is a thought with an unclear speaker (keep it with the narrator). Two separate shots would break a line that continues over the cut (the timeline decides). |
| Evidence | 7434a0f7, 3.0-4.5 s: "the women figre hand mve and then the male figre hand move" (owner, B04). 7434a0f7, 5.0-6.5 s: "first the maharaj hand move and the standing char move right after that" (owner, B05). The frames show the curse gesture, then the refusal ("So she cursed" / "He said no"). |
| Verification level | H1 (owner's words on both items); S consistent. |
| GranthStudio compatibility | COMPATIBLE, translated: the reference animates both moves inside one shot, which GranthStudio cannot do. Use two held poses in shot order (cause in shot k, response in shot k+1, cut), or one held picture that already shows the response with its cause in frame. Presets 02 or 03 for the cause; 07 (0.14, no halo) or 09 (0.13) for the response. |
| Concrete scene example | S3 (97-98). Shot 1: Gajanan seated among the used leaf plates on the road, the two friends at the edge of frame (97, the sight). Shot 2: the same place, both friends in a held astonished pose facing him (98, "आश्चर्यचकित"). The astonishment pose is a STAGING claim for the owner to approve. |

## DP2. One stable frame for an exchange: change the pose, not the place

| Field | Content |
|---|---|
| What it helps achieve | Who is where stays clear through a conversation. The change in the scene is carried by the people's poses, not by new places. |
| When to use | Two or more people talk or argue in one place across several ovis (debate, admission, decision). |
| When NOT to use | The ovi moves the story to another time or place. In the reference, the composition changes at about 5 s, when the story jumps ("after 1000 years with the guru, Kacha was back", owner B05/B06). It is still OPEN whether that boundary at 4.93 s is a cut or a lighting effect, so rely on the content change, not the transition type. |
| Evidence | 7434a0f7, 0.5-4.5 s: the same backdrop, both figures full length, the exchange played in one composition. B02 "approve" (owner). B03 and B04: slow zoom-in, no pan (owner, then confirmed paste). |
| Verification level | H1 + H2 for the camera; S for "same backdrop, full figures". |
| GranthStudio compatibility | COMPATIBLE. Keep one setting: 09_dialogue_composition (left/right figures, 0.13) or 02 (layered, 0.13) or 03 on one full image (0.20). Change the pose image between shots. Keep the same scenery layers. |
| Concrete scene example | S6 (119-121). Shot 1 wide at Devidas's door, Bankatlal turned to Damodarpant (119). Shot 2: 09 with the same two figures, Bankatlal continuing his address (120). Shot 3: 03 on a full doorway image with Bankatlal in a concluding pose and Gajanan seated (121). |
| Open point (owner) | Is a spoken comparison (Arjuna and Dwarka, 120) a change of story place? The reference gives no evidence about comparisons. The validator allows the cutaway (F-16 in the inventory). Until the owner rules, this principle is NOT evidence for or against picturing comparisons. |

## DP3. Reveal a newcomer into a frame that was held without them

| Field | Content |
|---|---|
| What it helps achieve | An arrival reads as an event: the viewer sees the place before and after the person comes. |
| When to use | An ovi states that someone arrives or appears (comes back, enters, is seen for the first time). |
| When NOT to use | The person is already in the scene. The ovi does not state an arrival (do not stage one). The arrival would need walking inside a shot. |
| Evidence | 7434a0f7, 9.0 s: wide/full shot of Kacha alone (owner-confirmed B08). 9.5-11.0 s: "Devayani walks in from the left with her arm already held out"; reveal YES (owner-confirmed B09). Her red forehead mark is already visible at 9.0 s, so nothing is "gained" during the shot (B10). |
| Verification level | H2. |
| GranthStudio compatibility | PARTIAL. No walking inside a shot. Do it as a cut between two held images in the same framing ("before" without the person, "after" with them already in place), or as one 02 parallax pan from the person present to the arriving one. 04_character_entrance exists (walking pose and pan) but carries the fixed halo. |
| Concrete scene example | S8 (135). Shot k: Gajanan beside the trough after drinking, alone (135, first half). Shot k+1: the same well-side framing with Damodarpant now standing there holding the gadva (135, "घेऊन आले पंत पाणी गडव्यांत"). Both stored plans instead used one parallax pan across both figures; that is also allowed. |

## DP4. Save the strongest framing change for the turn

| Field | Content |
|---|---|
| What it helps achieve | The beat that changes the scene stands out. The other beats stay calm, so the emphasis means something. |
| When to use | One ovi is clearly the scene's turn (a decision, an offer, a refusal) and a closer image of the speaker or the key object is supported by the source. |
| When NOT to use | The turn's speaker is marked unclear (S3 103-104, S6 122): a one-face close-up would assign the speaker, so keep both faces or stay wide. The turn exists only in narration. Do not use 08 (fixed halo) where the ovi gives no divine cause (F4); prefer 07 or 10. Do not stack several strong moves before it. |
| Evidence | 7434a0f7: from about 11.7 s the frame goes from wide to medium at "Your vow is over. Marry me." and back to wide at about 12.9 s (owner-confirmed B10; the paste calls it "a strong zoom ... not slight"). Earlier beats have only a slow zoom-in (B03, B04). |
| Verification level | H2. |
| GranthStudio compatibility | PARTIAL. Zoom gains are fixed, so emphasis comes from choosing a closer image plus a preset: 10_object_hand_insert (0.14) for an object, 07_character_reaction (0.14) for a face, 08 (0.25, halo) only when the halo is acceptable. The other shots use 02, 03 or 09. |
| Concrete scene example | S8 (136-137). Keep shots 1-3 wide or two-figure. Make the 137 offer the one close shot: 10_object_hand_insert on the gadva with the clear water and vetiver (137 "गोड निर्मळ थंडगार ... वाळा"). Do not add a second close-up after it. |

## Held in reserve (not recommended now)

**DP5. Detail first, then the wide, when an object is the subject.**
- Evidence: 7a1e2b26, 0-1.88 s extreme close-up of the drill bit; 2.0-3.0 s hands on the handle; 3.5-4.0 s first wide shot of the boat (owner R2, S01-S03, H3).
- Why held back:
  - A weaker form of this idea was tested as lesson L1.
  - In S3 the lessons arm opened without establishing the place, and both AI raters preferred the plan that established it.
  - That preference is AI opinion from a single draw, but it is the only test there is.
  - Use it only for scenes whose subject is an object, never as a default opening.
